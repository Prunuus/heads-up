# v1 results (Task 12)

**v1 = the layered PolicyBot (T11) with the T10 v1 heuristic values, and Monte Carlo spent on equity only.**
- **The tuner proposed a different parameter set, and it was rejected.** On fresh disjoint seeds the 11-parameter bundle raised the points objective by only +0.002. It lost **−0.140 [−0.225, −0.055] bb/deal**, so by the rule fixed before the confirmation ran it was not adopted.
- **What did change behaviour.** Dropping the unused `now` rollouts (T10 review M7) is a measured gain: **+0.133 [+0.060, +0.206] bb/deal** at the primary 2.5× condition.
- **M1 gate: passed** at s = 2.5 and s = 1.0 in the quiet 2-worker run (20/20 baseline CI wins, 0 errors, max bank 1.27 s, HOLDOUT within 10% of TRAIN). **But v1 loses to all three FIELD proxies** (Concerns).

All runs were pinned to CPUs 0–7, sharing them with Task 14's 2 workers at nice 10, while CPUs 8–15 carried other tracks' jobs. Load averages are given with each timing number.

## Tuning run

**Budget: 100 candidate evaluations.**
- Each evaluation: 15 TRAIN opponents × 2 seeds × 500 deals at s = 2.5, so 30 matches.
- Batches rotate through a pool of 8 seeds (t1–t8). `random_frac` 0.4: 41 random candidates, then 59 coordinate-descent evaluations (1.4 passes). `min_gain` 0.005.
- **Wall time: 39 min** (2,359 s) with 6 workers, `taskset -c 0-7`, at load 15–24. CPUs 0–7 were shared with Task 14's 2 workers, and CPUs 8–15 were full of other tracks' jobs.

**Why that budget.**
- The plan's `--budget 200 --seeds 4 --deals 500` costs about 78 CPU-s per candidate-seed against 15 TRAIN opponents (measured), so about 6–7 h. The brief caps the run itself at about 90 min, and the confirmation and gate need the rest of the time box.
- 2 seeds × 500 deals is about 13 s wall per candidate-seed on 6 workers. 100 evaluations fit in about 40 min with a full first pass of coordinate descent.
- The fresh-seed confirmation guards against a noisy proposal.

**Command.**
`taskset -c 0-7 .venv/bin/python -m sim.tune --budget 100 --seeds 8 --batch-seeds 2 --seed-prefix t --deals 500 --workers 6 --time-scale 2.5 --random-frac 0.4 --min-gain 0.005 --rng-seed 12`

- Coordinate descent accepted 10 moves in 22 batches:

  It kept `open_min`, `range_weight`, `bet_range` and `thin_value`. In pass 2, `open_min`, `threebet_min`, `call_margin`, `range_discount` and `value_raise` each held against both neighbours on new seeds.
- **Noise.** The incumbent's own score ranged 0.960–1.026 across seed pairs, and within-batch differences between candidates were about ±0.015–0.02, above `min_gain`. So the descent partly random-walked, which is why the proposal had to be confirmed on fresh seeds.

A single-process probe at load 20 measured a 46 ms worst GC pause and an 11–15 ms worst request, so I read these as machine stalls under load 20–24 (each also inflated that half's bank to 3.6 s). **Readings.**
- **The T11 grace works without being told the CPU speed.** With warmup and the online estimate it reaches the oracle level (4.4–5.3 s), well below the 7.1 s of the default grace.
- **The online estimate alone catches a slow CPU** that warmup missed (the cold arm).
- **The busy-wait arms under-count by 5–11%.** That is the known 0.25 vs 0.29 ms plumbing constant (T11 review ⚠️2), about 0.1 ms per request at f = 5. The 6 s hard stop absorbs it. I did not re-fit the constant: there was no clean measurement to fit it to (see the contention readings above), and the time box was short.
- **In the time_scale arm the bot over-counts,** as designed.

## Concerns

1. **v1 loses to all three FIELD proxies** (above). It is the same heuristic as the failsafe `ff537d2`. Static tuning did not fix the thin adaptive margins: the confirmed tuned bundle was no better against ml_minded or gto_inspired. This needs Task 14's exploit layer, or a mixed strategy (v3), and a decision on what to submit.
2. **Static tells** (method section): POT-sized bets are always strong, there are no out-of-position bluffs, and there are no semi-bluff raises. field_exploit gains on v1 within a half.
3. **The bot under-counts the judge by about 30% when its CPU is contended** (real-judge readings above). It is absorbed by the 3 s target and the 6 s stop vs the 8 s bank, but the grace model is weakest there.
4. **The tuner's noise floor.** At 2 seeds × 500 deals the within-batch noise (±0.015–0.02) is above every single-parameter effect it could find. Only a much larger budget per candidate, or a narrower space, would let tuning beat the T10 values.

## Method

### The emulated tournament CPU (ruling R30b)

`sim.match.play_half` / `play_match` take a per-bot `time_scale` s (one number for both bots, or a pair). It emulates a CPU s times slower than the dev PC.

- **What the judge would charge.** Every request's measured time is multiplied by s before it is charged to the bank and compared with the timeouts (1 s per request, and the bank's remainder).
- **The free time per request follows s.** The default grace is `grace_for_time_scale(s)` = the judge's free 1 ms minus 0.25 ms × s of HTTP plumbing, which the in-process sim does not run. That is 0.75 ms at s = 1 (the old default), 0.375 ms at s = 2.5, and negative past s = 4 (the judge charges plumbing overrun).
- **What each bot sees.** A bot that times itself would see the slow CPU too, so each bot is told:
  - Bots receive `SimAgentConfig` (an `AgentConfig` with an extra `time_scale` field). The real judge's plain `AgentConfig` has no such field, so the shipped bot reads 1.0 in the tournament.
  - A bot file's module-level `time` (what every llm zoo bot budgets by: `time.perf_counter()`) is replaced by `sim.match.ScaledTime`, whose clocks measure every interval s times as long. Sleeping is not sped up.
  - The archetype bots do fixed work and keep no clock: they spend as on this PC and are charged as on the slow CPU. That is exactly what a fixed-work bot does on slow hardware.
- **How our bot composes with it** (`bot/policy.py`):
  - `PolicyBot.new_half` reads `config.time_scale` (`sim_time_scale`) and gives its TimeManager a clock that runs s times fast. Decisions, `on_hand_end` and the online CPU samples therefore measure what they would on such a CPU.
  - The warmup CPU factor was measured at this PC's speed, so it is multiplied by s.
  - The grace is 1 ms − 0.25 ms × max(s × warmup factor, online factor). Both factors are ≥ s, so the bot's grace never exceeds the sim's, and its own count stays at or above the sim's charge, as with the real judge.
  - In-process sims read online factors of about 1–2.5 × s (T11), so the bot over-counts in sims. That is the safe direction.

Why the opponent clock matters: without `ScaledTime`, at s = 2.5 six of the 15 TRAIN opponents ran out of bank inside a 500-deal half. That included wall-clock-adaptive bots (beginner_2h, opp_model, ml_minded at 7.93 s), which on real slow hardware would see their own time and pace themselves. With it, at 1,000 deals, the adaptive bots end their halves at 2.5–5.1 s. Only the fixed-work bots (mc_equity_fast/slow, checkfold_after_300, simple_rules) still bank out, which is what slow hardware does to them.

**Primary condition: s = 2.5** for tuning and for the gate. s = 1.0 is reported too.

### The tuner (`sim/tune.py`)

`tune(param_space, opponents="train", seeds, deals, budget, ...) -> HeuristicParams`. Candidates are `functools.partial(PolicyBot, params)` (ruling R5), played in one persistent multiprocessing pool.

**Objective (D1): expected points + 0.01 × mean bb/deal.** For each opponent, the per-deal results of all seeds in a batch are pooled into a mean m and a standard deviation sd. The expected points are those of a 1,000-deal tournament match under the judge's verdict rule: P(win) + 0.5 · P(chop), where P(win) = Φ(m/se − 1.96), P(loss) = Φ(−m/se − 1.96) and se = sd/√1000.
- Why not the verdicts of the tuning matches themselves? A 500-deal tuning match scores the points of a *500-deal* match, which is the wrong match length, and it moves in steps of 0.5.
- The estimator is the quantity the tournament pays, and it is smooth in m. Clear wins saturate at 1, so a crushable bot cannot dominate the objective; borderline opponents carry the signal.
- The raw verdict points are logged next to it.

**Search.**
1. *Random search.* One batch of the base (T10 v1 defaults) plus random neighbours of it. Each parameter moves to another value of its list with probability 3/14.
2. *Coordinate descent* from the best of that batch. For each parameter in turn, a batch of the incumbent plus its neighbouring values (one step either way in its list).
   - A challenger replaces the incumbent only if it beats it by more than `min_gain` in the same batch.
   - Passes repeat until one pass moves nothing or the budget runs out.
- **Seeds (T10 review M8).** Every candidate in a batch plays the same seeds (common random numbers, so comparisons are paired). Consecutive batches take consecutive pairs of seeds from a pool of 8 (t1–t8). The search therefore sees a broad seed pool, and the incumbent is re-measured on fresh seeds in every batch, which damps the winner's curse.
- **Validation (M3).** Every candidate is a `HeuristicParams`, whose constructor rejects non-finite values and short tuples. The space is validated before any match.
- **The space.** 3 values for each of the 14 fields, with the T10 v1 default in the middle. Per-street fields move all three streets together. `nonnut_penalty` is in the space (M6).

### Confirmation protocol (fixed before the confirmation ran)

- **Seeds.** Fresh and disjoint: k1–k8 for confirmation, g1–g5 for the M1 gate. Tuning used t1–t8; earlier tasks used s, q, m, p, e and t11a.
- **Opponents.** TRAIN only for every decision. HOLDOUT is used only by the gate.
- **Arms on common seeds.** T10 v1 defaults (`default`), the tuner's proposal (`tuned`), and the T10 v1 defaults with the old `now` rollouts (`nowmc`, the M7 ablation). Each plays 1,000-deal matches at s = 2.5.
- **Adoption rule for the tuned set.** Adopt it only if, on k1–k8:
  - its objective (the tuner's, from 8,000 pooled deals per opponent) beats `default`'s, **and**
  - the pooled paired per-deal bb difference tuned − default does not have its whole 95% CI below 0.

  Otherwise v1 keeps the T10 v1 values.
- **Ablation rule for `now`.** Keep the rollouts dropped unless `default` − `nowmc` is significantly negative.

### Real-judge and in-container validation (T11 review ⚠️3)

- **Our bot.** The `docker/Dockerfile.agent` image built from the committed tree, run with check_build's restrictions: read-only files, a 512 MB `/tmp` tmpfs, no capabilities, `no-new-privileges`, 256 pids, `--cpus 1`, 4 GB, CPUs 0–7. Its port is reachable so the host judge can talk to it.
  - Default path: the judge reaches the container on its bridge IP (kernel path, like pod networking).
  - One match also goes through `-p 127.0.0.1:5008` (docker-proxy), to show what an extra hop costs.
- **Opponent and judge.** `baseline-bots/*.py` and `judge_engine.py --half {1,2}`, on the host, pinned to CPUs 0–7.
- **Fresh servers.** Both bots get new processes for every half (README section 4). `judge_engine.py combine` pairs the halves.
- **Instrumentation.** The container runs `agent.py` unchanged through a read-only probe mount (`probe_agent.py`, never in the image). At the end of each half the probe prints what the bot measured:
  - the warmup CPU factor and the online CPU factor;
  - its own bank count and its grace;
  - layer faults and failed warmup steps.

### 5× CPU emulation with the T11 grace (the T10 review's method)

- **The slowdown.** Every `compute_features` / `heuristic_action` call busy-waits 4× its own time, inside the bot's timed region. Both the TimeManager and the T11 online CPU estimate therefore see a 5× CPU.
- **The sim's grace.** 1 ms − 0.29 ms × 5 = −0.45 ms (this PC's plumbing, scaled).
- **Setup.** Shipped PolicyBot, seed `slow1`, half 1, 1,000 deals, vs calling_station and llm_ml_minded, as in the review.
- **Arms.**
  - *warm*: the warmup factor is multiplied by 5 too (a real slow CPU slows warmup as well).
  - *cold*: warmup reads this PC, so only the online estimate can see the slowdown.
  - *scale*: no busy-wait; `time_scale=5` instead.

### Static tells (user info: adaptive opponents) — considered, not changed in v1

v1's betting is a pure function of its equity estimates, so its sizes carry information:
- **Postflop RAISE_POT.** Only when the effective equity is above `pot_vs_half_cut`, or with the river nuts. It is never a bluff and never thin value.
- **RAISE_HALF.** Covers thin value, value below the cut, and the in-position bluffs.
- **Out of position.** v1 never bluffs: an OOP bet is always at least `thin_value`.
- **Facing a bet.** It raises only above `value_raise`, with no semi-bluff raises.
- **Preflop.** The open size follows strength: pot at `threebet_min` or above, half otherwise.

**What mixing would cost and gain.**
- Remedies: pot-sized bluffs, half-pot nut bets, and some OOP bluffs.
- Cost: they lose chips against the zoo, whose adaptive bots (ml_minded adapts to our fold-to-raise rate) do not read sizes.
- Benefit: robustness against a size-reading field bot, which the zoo cannot measure. So no in-zoo experiment could justify the cost.
- The tuner did move `bluff_freq`; see the confirmation.

**Where this belongs instead.** Task 14's reversible, confidence-gated exploit modes and v3's blueprint (mixed by construction). The first-500 vs last-500 check below measures whether any adaptive bot actually gains on v1 within a half.
